Title: Chapterhouse: Dune
Author: Frank Herbert
Year Published: 1985
Genre: Science Fiction / Apocalyptic Space Opera / Matriarchal Warfare / Survival Thriller
Pacing: Tense, strategic, desperate war of survival, leading to a legendary cosmic cliffhanger
Series: The Dune Chronicles
Series Part: Book 6 of 6 (The Original Frank Herbert Hexalogy)
Series Order: 6
Preceded By: Heretics of Dune (Book 5)
Followed By: Hunters of Dune (Dune 7 by Brian Herbert & Kevin J. Anderson)
Universe Category: Original Canon (Frank Herbert's Final Work)
Chronological Context: Direct continuation of Heretics of Dune. Arrakis has been vaporized by weaponized Obliterators; the Bene Gesserit are transforming their secret headquarters planet (Chapterhouse) into a new desert.
Target Audience: Fans who want the thrilling culmination of Frank Herbert's universe, philosophical debates on survival and culture, and the setup to the great cosmic mystery of Dune 7.

Vibes:
- The ancient home planet Arrakis is gone, leaving only a single smuggled sandworm to save the spice cycle
- Transforming an apple-orchard green world (Chapterhouse) into a scorched desert planet
- High-stakes psychic conditioning, ghola awakenings, and weaponized love
- A mysterious elderly couple tending a cosmic net in deep space

Spoiler-Free Premise:
Following the catastrophic planetary destruction of Arrakis by the ferocious Honored Matres, the Bene Gesserit Sisterhood faces complete extermination. With only a handful of fortress worlds remaining, Mother Superior Darwi Odrade enacts a desperate gambit: Chapterhouse, the secret capital of the Sisterhood, is being systematically terraformed into a barren desert to nurture the last surviving sandworm and resurrect the spice cycle. As the armada of Honored Matres closes in for the final slaughter, Odrade attempts an impossible synthesis of the two rival orders, while Duncan Idaho and an enigmatic Tleilaxu master glimpse a chilling, omnipresent menace watching from the infinite void.

Reading & Buying Decision Guide:
- Genre Deconstruction: Pure apocalyptic strategic thriller combined with Frank Herbert's ultimate reflections on institutional survival, ethics, and love as a survival mechanism.
- The Famous Cliffhanger: This was Frank Herbert's final novel before his passing in 1986. The novel concludes on a major, spine-tingling cliffhanger involving an elusive cosmic danger.
- Series Continuity: Concludes Frank Herbert's original 6-book saga! Readers who want the resolution to the cliffhanger can proceed to Hunters of Dune and Sandworms of Dune.
