Title: Dune: House Atreides
Author: Brian Herbert and Kevin J. Anderson
Year Published: 1999
Genre: Science Fiction / Prequel / Galactic Political Thriller / Space Opera
Pacing: Fast, intrigue-heavy, rich backstory, alternating between diverse aristocratic perspectives
Series: Prelude to Dune Trilogy
Series Part: Book 1 of 3 (Prelude to Frank Herbert's 1965 masterpiece)
Series Order: Prequel Trilogy Book 1
Preceded By: Legends of Dune Trilogy (Chronologically earlier in ancient history)
Followed By: Dune: House Harkonnen (Prelude Book 2)
Universe Category: Expanded Dune Universe (Brian Herbert & Kevin J. Anderson)
Chronological Context: Takes place approximately 35 years before the events of Frank Herbert's original Dune.
Target Audience: Readers who fell in love with the world of Dune and want to see how Duke Leto grew up, how Baron Harkonnen came to power, and the secret origins of Lady Jessica and Planetologist Pardot Kynes.

Vibes:
- The youthful days of Duke Leto Atreides on Caladan facing early assassination attempts
- The decadent court of Padishah Emperor Elrood IX and Crown Prince Shaddam IV on Kaitain
- Imperial planetary ecologist Pardot Kynes first arriving on Arrakis and discovering the Fremen
- Secret Bene Gesserit breeding programs and Harkonnen spice manipulations

Spoiler-Free Premise:
Set nearly four decades before the events of Frank Herbert's classic Dune, House Atreides explores the formative years of the characters whose destinies would change the galaxy. On the water planet Caladan, nineteen-year-old Leto Atreides prepares to succeed his charismatic father, Duke Paulus. On the desert world of Arrakis, imperial planetologist Pardot Kynes is dispatched to unlock the planet's ecological secrets, only to become a revered prophet among the native Fremen. Meanwhile, on Kaitain, Crown Prince Shaddam IV schemes to murder his elderly father, the Padishah Emperor, while plotting with the Bene Tleilax to synthesize artificial spice and destroy the Spacing Guild's monopoly.

Reading & Buying Decision Guide:
- Genre & Style: Accessible, character-driven political intrigue and galactic world-building. Acts like a grand historical chronicle of the Dune universe.
- Series Continuity: While chronologically set before Dune, it is best enjoyed by readers who have already read Frank Herbert's 1965 original Dune so that the foreshadowing, dramatic irony, and character origins hit with full impact.
- Prequel Trilogy: Part 1 of the Prelude to Dune trilogy (House Atreides, House Harkonnen, House Corrino).
