Title: The Hobbit
Author: J.R.R. Tolkien
Year Published: 1937
Genre: Classic Fantasy / High Fantasy / Epic Adventure
Pacing: Whimsical yet steadily escalating from charming folklore to grand epic warfare
Target Audience: Readers of all ages who enjoy classic quests, rich mythological folklore, riddles, dragons, and tales of unlikely heroes finding courage.

Vibes:
- Cozy beginnings in the Shire turning into perilous mountain wilderness
- Ancient dwarven treasures, riddles in the dark, and sleeping dragons
- Old-world English storytelling tone, poetic and immersive
- Deep foundational roots of Middle-earth

Spoiler-Free Premise:
Bilbo Baggins is a quiet, comfort-loving hobbit who lives contentedly in his underground hole in Bag End, desiring nothing more than six meals a day and peace of mind. His quiet life is upended when the wizard Gandalf and a company of thirteen dwarves led by Thorin Oakenshield recruit Bilbo as their "burglar" on a quest to reclaim the Lonely Mountain and its vast hoard of gold from the fearsome dragon Smaug.

Reading & Buying Decision Guide:
- Romance Level: None.
- Complexity: Gentle and accessible. Much lighter and more whimsical than The Lord of the Rings trilogy.
- Violence / Trigger Notes: Mild fairy-tale and epic fantasy combat (giant spiders, goblins, wargs, battles).
- Series Information: Prequel to The Lord of the Rings trilogy. Works perfectly as a complete standalone novel.
- Common Questions:
  * "Do I need to read Lord of the Rings first?" No! The Hobbit was published first and serves as the ideal introduction to Middle-earth.
